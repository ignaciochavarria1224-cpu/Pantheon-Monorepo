## Part 1: Research Architecture

Apex Adapt is no longer a parameter optimizer. It is a **hypothesis-driven strategy research pipeline** with four discrete stages, each with explicit pass/fail gates. Nothing advances without clearing its gate.

### Stage 1: Hypothesis Generation

Every strategy begins as a written thesis about **participant behavior**, not a price pattern. The hypothesis must answer three questions before any code is written:

1. **Who is being exploited?** — Identify the specific market participant whose predictable behavior creates the edge. For Variant B: institutional execution desks benchmarked to VWAP, who must buy pullbacks on trending days to minimize their execution shortfall.
2. **Why do they keep doing it?** — The behavior must be structurally compelled, not discretionary. VWAP-benchmarked algos can't stop buying pullbacks — their mandate requires it. This is a durable edge because it comes from the structure of institutional execution, not from a market anomaly that can be arbitraged away.
3. **Under what conditions does the behavior manifest?** — The regime conditions where the behavior produces a tradeable signal. This is where most strategies fail: they assume the behavior is always present. The hypothesis must specify when it is and isn't active.

The output of Stage 1 is a **Strategy Hypothesis Document** (a plain text spec) that contains: the behavioral thesis, the regime conditions, the predicted market signature (what it should look like in data), and the falsification criteria (what evidence would disprove it).

### Stage 2: Strategy Modules

Each strategy is decomposed into four independent, testable modules:

- **Regime Classifier** — Takes raw market data, outputs a binary or categorical label for the current session. For Variant B: "trending day" or "not trending day." This module must be testable in isolation. You should be able to run it across 500 sessions and evaluate whether its classifications are accurate and stable before you ever connect it to an entry signal.

- **Signal Generator** — Given that the regime classifier has fired, the signal generator identifies the specific entry setup. For Variant B: first pullback to VWAP after the opening drive, with pullback depth and candle quality filters. This module consumes the regime label and raw price/volume data and outputs a candidate trade with a timestamp and direction.

- **Confirmation Gate** — An independent filter that accepts or rejects the candidate trade from the signal generator. This is NOT a redundant check — it evaluates a different dimension of the setup. For Variant B: pullback depth relative to opening drive, and candle body ratio on the reclaim bar. The confirmation gate exists to kill marginal setups that the signal generator identifies but that lack structural conviction.

- **Exit Engine** — Manages the trade once entered. This module is completely decoupled from entry logic. It receives the entry price, the current market state, and the regime context, and outputs hold/exit decisions on each new bar. For Variant B: two-tier trailing exit with a regime-invalidation hard stop.

The reason for modular decomposition is testability. If the full strategy underperforms, you can diagnose which module is degrading. If the regime classifier is accurate but the signal generator produces too many false positives, you know exactly where to intervene. Monolithic strategies — where entry, confirmation, and exit are tangled together — make this impossible.

### Stage 3: In-Sample Testing Protocol

IS testing has one purpose: **determine if the behavioral thesis produces a measurable statistical signature in historical data.** It is NOT for optimization.

The IS test protocol:

1. Run the regime classifier across the full IS dataset. Record its output labels.
2. For every session labeled as "trending day," manually inspect a sample of 30-50 sessions to verify the classification is correct. If the classifier is labeling rotational days as trending, the strategy will fail regardless of everything downstream.
3. Run the signal generator only on regime-classified sessions. Record every candidate trade.
4. Apply the confirmation gate. Record acceptance/rejection rates and the characteristics of rejected trades.
5. Run the exit engine on confirmed trades. Record per-trade P&L, holding time, MAE (maximum adverse excursion), and MFE (maximum favorable excursion).

Key IS metrics (in order of importance):

| Metric | What It Tells You | Minimum Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Win rate on regime-classified days only | Whether the thesis works in the right conditions | > 52% |
| Avg winner / Avg loser ratio | Whether the payoff profile is asymmetric | > 1.3 |
| Signal frequency | Whether there are enough trades for statistical significance | > 2 per week on average |
| MAE distribution | Whether losing trades blow through stops or get stopped cleanly | 80%+ of losses should be < 1.5× the stop distance |
| Regime classifier accuracy (manual audit) | Whether you're even trading in the right environment | > 70% agreement with manual review |
| Profit factor | Gross profits / gross losses — but only meaningful with 100+ trades | > 1.15 |

**Critical rule**: If profit factor is above 1.4 in-sample, be suspicious, not excited. Extremely high IS performance almost always indicates overfitting to a favorable stretch. A strategy with a genuine structural edge typically shows a modest IS profit factor (1.1–1.3) with high consistency, not explosive returns with variance.

### Stage 4: Out-of-Sample Validation and Promotion/Rejection

OOS validation uses a completely held-out dataset that was never seen during hypothesis development, classifier tuning, or IS testing.

**Promotion criteria** (ALL must be met):

1. OOS profit factor ≥ 0.90 × IS profit factor. Some degradation is expected and normal. A 10% drop is acceptable. A 30%+ drop indicates overfitting.
2. OOS win rate is within 5 percentage points of IS win rate.
3. OOS expectancy (avg P&L per trade) remains positive.
4. The distribution shape of per-trade returns in OOS resembles IS. Plot both as histograms — if IS has a smooth bell curve with a right skew and OOS has a bimodal or fat-left-tail distribution, the strategy is regime-dependent and fragile.
5. No single OOS month accounts for more than 40% of total OOS profit. If it does, the strategy's edge is episodic, not persistent.

**Rejection triggers** (ANY one kills the strategy):

- OOS expectancy is negative
- OOS max drawdown exceeds 2× IS max drawdown
- OOS profit factor < 1.0
- More than 3 consecutive OOS months are unprofitable

**Quarantine zone**: If the strategy passes promotion criteria but with marginal metrics (e.g., OOS PF between 1.0 and 1.05), it enters a paper-trade quarantine for 30 live sessions before being promoted to execution. This is where APEX (your execution agent) runs the strategy with zero capital risk to see if live-market performance matches OOS.

## Part 2: Variant B — Implementation-Ready Strategy Specification

### Strategy Name: VWAP Regime-Conditioned Momentum (VRC-M)

### Behavioral Thesis
On genuine trending days, institutional execution algorithms benchmarked to VWAP will systematically buy pullbacks to VWAP to minimize their execution shortfall. This creates predictable demand at VWAP on the first pullback after a strong opening drive. The edge comes from front-running this institutional flow, not from VWAP as a technical level.

---

### Regime Definition: Trending Day Classifier

A session qualifies as a trending day candidate if ALL THREE of the following conditions are true within the first 30 minutes of the session (9:30–10:00 ET):

**Condition R1 — Gap Outside Prior Value Area:**
The SPY open price is above the prior session's Value Area High (VAH) for a long-biased trending day, or below the prior session's Value Area Low (VAL) for a short-biased trending day.

*Why this exists:* When a market opens outside the prior session's established value zone, it signals that overnight information has shifted the fair value estimate. This creates a directional obligation for participants to reprice. Openings inside the prior value area suggest continuation of the prior session's balance — no new directional information, no trend.

**Condition R2 — Opening Drive Magnitude:**
The range from the session open to the high (for longs) or low (for shorts) of the first 30 minutes exceeds 60% of the 20-day average daily range (ADR).

*Why this exists:* A strong opening drive indicates that the gap is being accepted, not faded. If the market gaps above the prior VAH but only moves a tiny amount in the first 30 minutes, it's likely a gap-fill play, not a trending day. The 60% ADR threshold ensures the opening move has enough energy to indicate genuine directional commitment.

How to compute ADR: For each of the prior 20 sessions, take High − Low. Average those 20 values. That's your 20-day ADR.

**Condition R3 — VWAP Slope Monotonicity:**
VWAP must be monotonically increasing (for longs) or monotonically decreasing (for shorts) during the entire first 30 minutes. No inflection point.

*Why this exists:* A monotonic VWAP slope means that volume-weighted average transaction prices are consistently rising. If VWAP oscillates or flattens during the open, it indicates two-sided activity and balance, not a one-directional trend. Monotonic VWAP is a proxy for one-sided order flow dominance.

How to check: Calculate VWAP at each 5-minute bar close from 9:30 to 10:00 (6 data points). For a long setup, each subsequent value must be ≥ the prior value. Any decrease (even by $0.01) disqualifies the session.

**If all three conditions are met, the session is classified as a trending day and the strategy is armed. If any condition fails, no trades are taken for the rest of the session.**

---

### Entry Rules

**E1 — Pullback to VWAP:**
After the regime classifier fires (at 10:00 ET or later), wait for price to pull back and touch VWAP. "Touch" is defined as the low of a 5-minute candle being within 0.05% of the current VWAP value (this accounts for the fact that price rarely lands exactly on VWAP to the penny).

*Why this exists:* This is the core signal — price returning to the institutional execution benchmark after a trending opening drive. The pullback creates the entry point where VWAP-benchmarked algos are structurally incentivized to buy.

**E2 — First Pullback Only:**
Only trade the FIRST pullback to VWAP after the opening drive. Ignore the second, third, and all subsequent pullbacks.

*Why this exists:* The first pullback has the highest information content. It represents the first test of whether the trend's opening drive will be defended. Subsequent pullbacks indicate that the trend is losing momentum and that VWAP is becoming a contested level rather than a defended one. Trading later pullbacks significantly degrades win rate in structural trend strategies.

**E3 — Trigger Bar:**
The entry is not taken the moment price touches VWAP. Wait for the first 5-minute candle that closes above VWAP after the touch. Enter at the close of that candle.

*Why this exists:* A touch without a reclaim close is a potential breakdown, not an entry. Requiring a close above VWAP confirms that the touch attracted buying, not that price is slicing through. This one bar of patience eliminates a large class of false signals where price briefly touches VWAP and then continues lower.

---

### Confirmation Rules

Both must be true at the time of the trigger bar close. If either fails, the trade is rejected.

**C1 — Pullback Depth ≤ 50% of Opening Drive:**
Measure the opening drive as: (30-min high) − (session open). Measure the pullback depth as: (30-min high) − (pullback low). The pullback depth must be ≤ 50% of the opening drive.

*Why this exists:* A shallow pullback indicates that selling pressure during the retracement is weak. The buyers who drove the opening move are not liquidating aggressively. A deep pullback (>50%) suggests that the opening drive may have been a false move or that significant counter-directional flow has entered. The 50% level is not a magic number — it's a structural threshold. Beyond 50%, the pullback starts to look like a reversal, not a retracement.

**C2 — Trigger Bar Body Ratio ≥ 60%:**
The body of the trigger bar (|close − open|) must be at least 60% of the bar's total range (high − low).

*Why this exists:* A strong-bodied candle indicates that the bar closed near its high, meaning buyers were in control for the entire bar. A wick-heavy candle (body < 60% of range) indicates indecision — price rallied but then gave back gains within the bar. A wick-heavy reclaim candle is a warning sign that the VWAP reclaim lacks conviction.

---

### Exit Rules

**X1 — Two-Tier Position Split:**
Divide the position into two halves at entry.

- **Half 1 — Range Projection Target:** Exit the first half when price reaches the entry price + (opening drive range). This is a 1:1 measured move from the reclaim point, using the opening drive's range as the projection.
- **Half 2 — Chandelier Trailing Stop:** Trail the second half with a stop at: (highest high since entry) − 2 × ATR(14-period, 5-minute bars). Recalculate on every new 5-minute bar.

*Why the two-tier split:* The first half captures a defined profit target based on the structural thesis (the opening drive range represents the market's established directional energy, so projecting that range from the reclaim is a reasonable target). The second half captures potential continuation if the trend extends beyond the initial projection. The trailing stop lets winners run without giving back excessive gains.

**X2 — Regime Invalidation Stop:**
Full exit of any remaining position if any 15-minute candle closes below VWAP.

*Why this exists:* The entire thesis is that VWAP will act as a defended level on a trending day. If a 15-minute candle closes below VWAP (not just a wick — a close), the thesis is invalidated. The institutional buying that was supposed to defend VWAP has failed. Staying in the trade after thesis invalidation is hope-based trading, not strategy-based trading. The 15-minute timeframe prevents a single noisy 5-minute candle from ejecting you.

**X3 — Time Stop:**
If neither X1's first target nor X2 has triggered within 90 minutes of entry, exit the full remaining position at market.

*Why this exists:* Trending day momentum is a morning phenomenon. The VWAP reclaim on a trending day should resolve relatively quickly — institutional flow is concentrated in the first half of the session. If the trade hasn't worked within 90 minutes, the thesis is not playing out as expected, and holding longer exposes you to afternoon mean reversion and noise.

## Part 3: Required Data Fields and Derived Features

Your current replay engine almost certainly has basic OHLCV at the 5-minute level. Here's what you need to add, organized by which module consumes them.

### For the Regime Classifier

| Field | Derivation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prior Session VAH | The upper boundary of the value area (70% of volume) from the prior complete session | Requires a volume profile computation per session. You need per-bar volume assigned to price levels, then find the range containing 70% of total session volume centered on the VPOC. |
| Prior Session VAL | The lower boundary of the value area from the prior complete session | Same computation as VAH. |
| Prior Session VPOC | The price level with the highest traded volume in the prior session | Needed later for exits and for Variant A if you ever build it. |
| 20-Day ADR | Rolling average of (session high − session low) for the prior 20 sessions | Simple derived feature. Recompute daily. |
| Opening Drive Range | (High of 9:30–10:00) − (Session open) for long setups | Computed at 10:00 ET. Stored as a session-level feature. |
| VWAP 5-Min Series | VWAP value at the close of each 5-minute bar | Running cumulative (price × volume) / cumulative volume from session open. You likely have this already. |
| VWAP Slope Check | Boolean: is the VWAP series monotonically increasing from 9:30 to 10:00? | Derived from the VWAP 5-min series. Simple comparison of 6 sequential values. |

### For the Signal Generator and Confirmation Gate

| Field | Derivation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| VWAP Proximity Flag | Boolean: did the low of the current 5-min bar come within 0.05% of current VWAP? | This is your "touch" detector. |
| Pullback Depth | (Opening drive high) − (lowest low since the opening drive high) | Track the running minimum after the 30-min high is established. |
| Pullback Depth Ratio | Pullback depth / Opening drive range | Must be ≤ 0.50 for confirmation. |
| Trigger Bar Body Ratio | |close − open| / (high − low) of the candle that closes above VWAP | Must be ≥ 0.60 for confirmation. |
| First Pullback Flag | Boolean: is this the first time price has touched VWAP since the opening drive? | Set to true on first touch, then permanently false for the rest of the session. |

### For the Exit Engine

| Field | Derivation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ATR (14-period, 5-min) | Standard average true range on 5-minute bars, 14-period lookback | For the chandelier trailing stop. Recalculate on every bar. |
| High-Water Mark | The highest high since trade entry | For trailing stop computation. Updates on each bar. |
| Chandelier Stop Level | High-water mark − (2 × ATR) | Recalculated every bar. Exit half 2 if price closes below this. |
| Range Projection Target | Entry price + Opening drive range | Static from entry. Exit half 1 when price ≥ this value. |
| 15-Min VWAP Close Check | Did the most recent completed 15-minute candle close below VWAP? | For the regime invalidation stop. You'll need to track 15-minute candle closes alongside your 5-minute primary timeframe. |
| Time Since Entry | Number of minutes elapsed since entry | For the 90-minute time stop. |

### Data You Do NOT Need (And Should Not Add)

- Tick-level data or bid/ask spread — valuable in theory but adds massive complexity for marginal gain at this stage
- Options flow or dark pool prints — same issue; interesting but premature
- News sentiment feeds — introduces a dependency that's hard to replay historically
- Any indicator with a tunable lookback period beyond the ones specified above — resist the urge to add RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, etc.

## Part 4: Build Order

This sequence is designed so that each step is independently testable before the next step depends on it. No step requires anything from a later step.

### Phase 1: Data Infrastructure (Build This First)

**Step 1 — Session-Level Feature Pipeline**

Build the pipeline that processes raw OHLCV data and produces session-level derived features: prior session VAH, VAL, VPOC, 20-day ADR, and opening drive range. This is pure data engineering with no strategy logic. Test it by running it across your full historical dataset and spot-checking 20 sessions manually against a charting platform. If your value area computations don't match what you see on a volume profile chart, everything downstream will be wrong.

**Step 2 — Intraday Derived Feature Pipeline**

Build the pipeline that produces bar-level features within a session: VWAP series, VWAP proximity flag, pullback depth tracker, pullback depth ratio, trigger bar body ratio, first pullback flag, ATR(14, 5-min), and 15-minute candle close tracker. Test this by running it on 10 known sessions and verifying every feature value against manual computation. This is tedious but essential — a bug in a derived feature will silently corrupt every backtest result.

### Phase 2: Regime Classifier (Build This Second)

**Step 3 — Trending Day Classifier**

Implement the three-condition regime classifier (R1, R2, R3) as a standalone module that takes session-level and intraday features as input and outputs a binary label per session. Run it across your full dataset. Examine the output distribution: what percentage of sessions get classified as trending days? If it's less than 15% or more than 40%, investigate. Trending days in SPY historically occur roughly 20-30% of sessions. If your classifier is wildly outside that range, one of the conditions is miscalibrated.

**Step 4 — Classifier Audit**

Manually review 50 sessions that the classifier labeled as trending days and 50 it labeled as non-trending. For each, pull up a chart and assess: does this actually look like a trending day? Compute the agreement rate. If the classifier agrees with your manual assessment less than 70% of the time, the regime definition needs revision before you proceed. Do NOT skip this step. A bad regime classifier will silently poison every downstream result.

### Phase 3: Signal and Confirmation Logic (Build This Third)

**Step 5 — Signal Generator**

Implement the entry logic (E1, E2, E3) as a module that runs only on regime-classified sessions and outputs candidate trades with timestamps. Run it and examine: how many candidate trades per week? What time of day do they cluster? Are there sessions with multiple false triggers (which shouldn't happen since you only take the first pullback)?

**Step 6 — Confirmation Gate**

Implement C1 and C2 as a filter on signal generator output. Measure the rejection rate: what percentage of candidate trades get killed by the confirmation gate? A healthy rejection rate is 20-40%. If the gate rejects less than 10%, it's not selective enough. If it rejects more than 60%, the signal generator is producing too many marginal setups and you may need to revisit the regime classifier's accuracy.

### Phase 4: Exit Engine (Build This Fourth)

**Step 7 — Exit Logic Implementation**

Implement all three exit rules (X1, X2, X3) as a module that takes an entry and the ongoing price stream and manages the position. The two-tier split must be handled correctly — track half 1 and half 2 separately with independent exit triggers. Test on 20 known trades by manually computing what the exits should be and comparing to the module's output.

### Phase 5: Full Integration and IS Testing (Build This Fifth)

**Step 8 — Integrated Backtest**

Connect all four modules (regime → signal → confirmation → exit) into a single pipeline and run the full IS backtest. Record every metric from the IS testing protocol defined in Part 1. This is the first time you see a profit factor and equity curve.

**Step 9 — IS Diagnostic Review**

Before celebrating or despairing over the IS results, run diagnostics. Examine the losing trades: are they clustered on specific dates? (If so, there may be a regime the classifier isn't catching.) Examine the winning trades: are they clustered? (If so, the edge may be episodic, not persistent.) Compute the metric table from Part 1 and evaluate against the thresholds.

### Phase 6: OOS Validation (Build This Last)

**Step 10 — OOS Test**

Run the complete pipeline on the held-out OOS dataset with zero modifications. Compare IS and OOS metrics using the promotion/rejection criteria from Part 1. This is a binary gate: the strategy either passes or it doesn't. If it fails, go back to the hypothesis and ask what structural assumption was wrong — do not start tweaking parameters.

**Step 11 — Paper Trade Quarantine (If Promoted)**

If the strategy passes OOS validation, deploy it in APEX in paper-trade mode for 30 live sessions. This is your final validation layer: does the strategy perform in real-time market conditions with realistic execution assumptions (slippage, partial fills, latency)?

## Summary Decision Tree

```
Hypothesis → Does the behavioral thesis make structural sense?
  └─ No  → Discard, generate new hypothesis
  └─ Yes → Build data pipeline
              └─ Build regime classifier → Audit it manually
                   └─ Accuracy < 70% → Revise regime definition
                   └─ Accuracy ≥ 70% → Build signal + confirmation
                        └─ IS backtest → Evaluate metrics
                             └─ PF < 1.05 → Thesis likely wrong, discard
                             └─ PF 1.05–1.40 → Proceed to OOS
                             └─ PF > 1.40 → Suspect overfitting, audit
                                  └─ OOS validation
                                       └─ Fails criteria → Discard
                                       └─ Passes criteria → Paper quarantine
                                            └─ Live performance degrades → Discard
                                            └─ Live performance holds → Promote to APEX
```
