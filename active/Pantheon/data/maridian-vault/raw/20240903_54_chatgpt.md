Please tell us something about yourself, your experiences, or activities -650w


One of the most defining experiences in my life was my trip to South korea. When I was 10 years old I spent a month in Korea doing a summer camp. I was without my parents, no phone, new people. I had no other choice but to adapt to the situation that was presented. Although I was young I wasn’t scared, did I miss my home, yes, but at the end of the day I was there to have fun. This summer camp was going to last four weeks. The very first weekend I got there I went to a host family's home. It was a mid size apartment in the center of Seoul, you could see the sunset behind the buildings from the balcony, truly majestic. This family took me to my first ever korean barbeque and I still have dreams about the Ssamjang. It's a thick spicy dipping sauce made from soybeans. The unique taste makes anything taste good. 

Walking the streets of South Korea is different from the streets in the United states. People are very quiet in South Korea, and their public transportation is extraordinary. The family that hosted me for the first weekend went out of their way to implement great hospitality. They took me to go watch movies, eat at authentic restaurants, and visit the most popular monuments. In one weekend we saw most of the city and used public transportation most of the time. After my time with the host family had come to an end, I went to the campgrounds. It was a medium size hotel secluded from the rest of South Korea, it had a soccer field, cafeteria, and auditorium. Groups of five from all around the world met up and spent the next four weeks in this place. In my room I was with two more boys, one from Norway and the other from mexico. I felt a connection with the kid from Mexico instantly because we both spoke spanish. I got to know Mateo and actually still communicate with him every once and a while. 

The camp consisted of multiple minigames everyday. We had a set schedule for eating, recess, and sleeping. Every delegation would have their day to wake up the rest, we would have to wake up a couple minutes earlier and make lots of noise to wake everyone up. Some delegations included devices from their culture, others played music from their country, everyone did something different. While at the camp I was observing other cultures that I would have never experienced if I wasn't there. And at the same time I was learning multiple lessons, due to the minigames and the wise people there. 

We thought we were waking up to a regular day, but then we realized that some of the kids got to sleep in while we had to wake up. We went to the lunch room and we had one option: bread. As we all sat down to eat our bread we noticed the leaders bringing in the kids that were allowed to sleep in stating “ The rich have arrived!”. They sat on chairs on the stage and people brought them food. This is when I realized what was happening, so as a smart 10 year old I threw a piece of bread at one of the leaders. I was beginning to learn, so I rebelled. I decided if enough of my peers joined me then something would have to change. So me and 12 other kids ended up in jail, the jail in the minigame was a room in the hotel,  before breakfast even ended, but we were standing up to them and we would not give up. At the end of the day we were released, but we learned a valuable lesson that day. Stand up for what you believe in, don’t let others tell you what you must believe.
