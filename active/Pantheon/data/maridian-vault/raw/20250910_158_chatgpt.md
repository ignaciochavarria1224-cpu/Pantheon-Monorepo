[Forms of communication in London]

One form of communication I have encountered in London is the writing on the streets that tell you to look left, right, or both ways. This is a form of external communication used to help tourists while crossing the street. The tone of the statements is a direct and instructive tone that leaves no room for misinterpretation. This really stood out to me for two reasons, the first being that in America I have never seen something like this, which makes sense due to the fact that we drive on the same side of the road as most other countries. Secondly, I use these a lot because I find myself confused when crossing the street. Overall, I believe this writing is a great depiction of communication in London. It is direct, practical and designed to be inclusive in a global city. It not only helps tourists while crossing the road but probably saves a lot of lives in the process. It also reminds me of the studies discussed in class such as the ones stating that people have about 2 seconds to read and process the words before getting on the subway, leading to the phrases being short and to the point. This helps explain the simple wording and bold white lettering stating “Look Left” or “Look Right”. It's short and simple enough for people to understand and be able to take action about it.
